1. Thinking is an obligation (*farīḍa*) that allows no concession and no suspension.
2. The mind was not created to be a guardian over transmitted material, accepting it with submission and blindness.
3. It was created to be a judge that distinguishes right from wrong.
4. It should measure matters by the standard of logic and proof.
5. The author calls it a crime (*jināya*).
6. It is committed first against oneself, before it wrongs the rights of others.
7. The author calls it a barrier to the path of knowledge.
8. That person loses the first distinctive quality for which humans were honored.
9. That person becomes unable to face civilizational challenges.
10. The human soul is asked for steadfastness and courage in climbing the ranks of understanding and awareness.
